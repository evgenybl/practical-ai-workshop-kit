Start here

In this session you will work through one invented case in four short exercises, then answer ten questions. Exercise 5 is homework. The planned time is 90 minutes, with your instructor.

One idea runs through all of it: the assistant drafts; you check; you decide. An assistant is fast at producing a first draft. It is not responsible for what you send. You are.

  • Keep the case in view. You will compare answers with the e-mails and the policy all session. Use the printed case from your instructor, or open this page in a second window and keep that one on The case.
  • The assistant your employer allows, or none. If you have none, or are not allowed one, every exercise that needs an assistant's answer has one ready for you: one model's recorded answer. Two answers in exercise 3 are edited, and say so.
  • Your answers stay here. What you type is kept in this browser, on this computer, and is sent nowhere. At the end, use Your answers to save a copy as a text file, or to clear everything.
  • The checks are helpers. Each Check button looks for simple signs and says what it found. It can miss things, and it can flag things that are fine. It points; you decide.
  • If this page will not work, open workbook-single-file.html, next to this folder: the same workbook in one file. The case is also in the case folder as plain files you can open or print.
  • Nothing here is real. The businesses, the people and the figures are invented. Never paste real customer or company data into this workbook.

Exercise 2: Summarise, and prove it

  
Planned: 20 minutes. Objective O2: ask for an answer that can be checked, and check it.

  
Goal: ask for a summary you can check, then check its figures and facts against the thread.

Step 3: Read, decide, and correct

  
For each Look again, open the thread and decide: keep it, fix it, or take it out. Then pick the three bullets you trust least and read each one against the message it cites, word by word: a sentence can pass every check and still say the opposite of the thread.

  
Last, write the version you would really send. In at most  words (message numbers in brackets are not counted), it must say:

  
    
    
  • the order number and the invoice number;
    • 
    
  • what went wrong: that the order came late, that cartons were missing, and that cartons arrived damaged;
    • 
    
  • the amount in question;
    • 
    
  • what was promised, including that 's promise to confirm a delivery day by  was not kept, and that no credit was promised.
    • 
  

  
The check looks for words, not meaning: for example late, missing, damaged, promised, Friday, did not. A due date followed by a later delivery date also counts as late. It does not count a word after "not" or "nothing".

  
  
  

    
  

  

  
What the check reads: every figure written in digits, compared as a number, so 1,250.00 and 1250 are the same figure. A date written as 28/02 is compared by its day and month. Message numbers in brackets, such as (msg 3), are read as citations, not as figures. A figure written in words, such as "six", is not checked.







  
Exercise 3: Find what is wrong

  
Planned: 15 minutes. Objective O3: name what is wrong in an answer that looks right.

  
Goal: find the errors in replies that read well.

  
In the session: answers A and B, eleven sentences. Answer C, further down, is for those who finish early, or homework.

  
Here are replies to 's last message, answering the strong brief of exercise 1. Answer A is one assistant's recorded reply, unchanged, with the assistant's own notes under it. Answer B is the same reply, edited to plant errors that A does not contain. The assistant's notes are not numbered, but read them. Keep the thread and the policy open. For each numbered sentence, choose one mark:

  
    
    
  • Fine: true to the thread, and allowed by the policy.
    • 
    
  • A wrong figure: a number that is wrong, or used with the wrong meaning.
    • 
    
  • Not in the material: a fact that is in neither the thread nor the policy.
    • 
    
  • Against the policy: a promise or an offer the policy does not allow.
    • 
  

  
Some sentences can be marked two ways. Pick the mark you can defend; the result says when the key accepts both.

Exercise 4: What must not go in

  
Planned: 20 minutes. Objective O4: decide what must not be pasted into a public assistant, and prepare a table so that it can be.

  
Goal: sort what may be pasted, and prepare a table so that nothing personal leaves this computer.

  
Your employer's own policy on AI tools comes first. If it is stricter than anything in this exercise, follow it.


  
Part 1: Sort the eight snippets

  
For each snippet, choose a pile: fine to paste, paste after removing details, or do not paste. Think about who could be harmed, and whether the question needs the detail at all. Some snippets can go in two piles; pick the one you can defend.
