Start here

In this session you will work through one invented case in five short exercises, then answer ten questions. The planned time is 90 minutes, with your instructor.

One idea runs through all of it: the assistant drafts; you check; you decide. An assistant is fast at producing a first draft. It is not responsible for what you send. You are.

  • Any assistant, or none. Use any chat assistant your employer allows. If you have none, or are not allowed one, every exercise that needs an assistant's answer has one ready for you.
  • Your answers stay here. What you type is kept in this browser, on this computer, and is sent nowhere. At the end, use Your answers to save a copy as a text file, or to clear everything.
  • The checks are helpers. Each Check button looks for simple signs and says what it found. It can miss things, and it can flag things that are fine. It points; you decide.
  • Nothing here is real. The businesses, the people and the figures are invented. Never paste real customer or company data into this workbook.

Exercise 1: From a wish to a brief

  
Planned: 15 minutes. Objective O1: write a request as a five-part brief.

  
Goal: turn a one-line wish into a brief that an assistant can act on.


  
Step 1: See what a wish produces

  
A colleague pasted the whole thread into an assistant, with one line above it:  Below is a reply to that request.

  

  

  
  
  

    
    
  

  

  


  
Step 2: Write the request again, in five parts

  
Write each part as if to a capable new colleague who has never heard of this customer. The colleague will do exactly what you ask, and only that.


  

    
    
Who are you, who is the reader, and what has happened? Why does it matter today?

    
  

  

    
    
Everything the assistant needs, and nothing it does not. Here: the whole thread. If your limits rely on the policy, add the policy too.

    

      
      
    

    
  

  

    
    
The one job, starting with a verb: draft, summarise, list.

    
  

  

    
    
How long, in what shape, in what tone. For example: "at most 150 words, in three short paragraphs".

    
  

  

    
    
What the answer must not do. Write at least one sentence that starts with "Do not".

    
  

  

    
  

  


  
Step 3, if you have an assistant: try your brief

  
Copy your brief as one text, send it, and paste the reply here. Check it, and compare it with the reply in step 1. What changed, and why?

  

    
    
  

  
  
  

    
  

  


  

    Compare with one strong brief
    
One way to write it. Yours can differ and still be strong: what matters is that each part does its job.

    

  







  
Exercise 2: Summarise, and prove it

  
Planned: 20 minutes. Objective O2: ask for an answer that can be checked, and check it.

  
Goal: get a summary for your manager that you can check, then check its figures and facts against the thread.


  
Step 1: Read the request

  
This request is ready to send. Look at its Form part: it asks the assistant to put the number of the message each fact comes from after the fact. That one line is what makes the answer quick to check.

  
  
  

    
    
  


  
Step 2: Get an answer, and check it

  
Send the request to your assistant and paste its answer below. No assistant, or not allowed one? Use the stand-in answer.

  

    
  

  

  
  
  

    
  

  

  
What the check reads: every figure written in digits, compared as a number, so 1,250.00 and 1250 are the same figure, and a date counts as its parts. Message numbers in brackets, such as (msg 3), are read as citations, not as figures. A figure written in words, such as "six", is not checked.


  
Step 3: Decide, and correct

  
For each Look again, open the thread, find the message, and decide: keep it, fix it, or take it out. Then write the version you would really send your manager, and check it again.

Exercise 4: What must not go in

  
Planned: 20 minutes. Objective O4: decide what must not be pasted into a public assistant, and prepare a table so that it can be.

  
Goal: sort what may be pasted, and prepare a table so that nothing personal leaves this computer.

  
Your employer's own policy on AI tools comes first. If it is stricter than anything in this exercise, follow it.


  
Part 1: Sort the eight snippets

  
For each snippet, choose a pile: fine to paste, paste after removing details, or do not paste. Think about who could be harmed, and whether the question needs the detail at all.
