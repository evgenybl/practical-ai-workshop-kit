Practical AI at Work

The assistant drafts; you check; you decide.

1. The five-part brief

Situation
Who you are, who will read the answer, what has happened, and why it matters now.
Material
Paste everything it needs, and nothing it does not. It knows only what you give it.
Task
One job, starting with a verb: draft, summarise, list.
Form
A length and a shape: "at most 150 words, in three short paragraphs". "Short" means different things to different people.
Limits
What it must not do, as plain sentences: "Do not promise a refund." "Use only the e-mails below."

Make it checkable: "After each fact, give the number of the message it comes from."

2. How to check an answer

  1. Find every figure in the material. Not there? It may be worked out, or made up. Check the sum, or take it out.
  2. Ask what each figure counts. The same number can mean two different things in one thread.
  3. Separate claims from facts. "She says she was promised" is not "we promised".
  4. Check every promise against your rules: money, dates, costs, anything that commits the company.
  5. Look for facts that are in neither the material nor your rules. Invented causes and offers read smoothly.
  6. A checker helps; it does not decide. A figure can be in the material and still be wrong. You send it, so you decide.

3. What must not go in

Fine to paste

  • Text that is already public
  • Your own words, meant to be published
  • Questions in general words

Paste after removing details

  • Replace names, contacts and numbers with stand-ins, such as Customer 01
  • Leave out what the question does not need
  • Keep the key on your own computer, and map the answer back yourself

Do not paste

  • Health or other sensitive facts about a person
  • Lists that are nothing but personal details
  • Confidential terms, contracts, passwords

Two questions before you paste: Does the question need this detail? Would the person it is about be comfortable seeing it here? Your employer's own policy comes first.
