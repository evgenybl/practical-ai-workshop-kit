# Rubric

Four levels for each of the five objectives, each described by **what you can see a participant do** during the exercise. Use it while you walk the room, to know what to look for and whom to help. It is for the instructor; participants are not scored, and the session does not depend on anyone reaching a level. This kit has not yet been taught to a class: the levels describe what the author planned to look for, not what was seen in a room.

| Level | In one line |
|---|---|
| **Not yet** | Does not do it, even with the workbook's prompt in front of them. |
| **Beginning** | Does part of it, or does it only when the checker points the way. |
| **Secure** | Does it unprompted, and can say why. This is the aim of the session. |
| **Strong** | Does it, explains it to others, and adapts it to a new situation. |

---

## O1. Write a request as a five-part brief (exercise 1)

| Level | What you can see |
|---|---|
| **Not yet** | Writes one line, or leaves boxes empty. Pastes no material, or only the customer's last message. Accepts the step 1 reply as sendable. |
| **Beginning** | Fills all five boxes, but the limits are wishes ("be careful with money") and the form is vague ("short"). Fixes them only after the check flags them. |
| **Secure** | All five parts do their job: the whole thread is pasted; the task starts with a verb; the form names a length or a structure; the limits include at least one "Do not ..." that would have prevented a problem in the step 1 reply. Can say why the material and the limits matter most. |
| **Strong** | Takes limits from the policy (who approves credits, no promised dates), states who the reader is and what they need, and gets a sendable reply at the first attempt. Helps a neighbour turn a wish into a limit. |

## O2. Ask for an answer that can be checked, and check it (exercise 2)

| Level | What you can see |
|---|---|
| **Not yet** | Adds no line to the request, or one such as "be accurate". Reads the summary and accepts it. Does not open the thread. Takes the checker's "OK" as "correct". |
| **Beginning** | Adds a line asking for sources only after the request check flags it. Fixes only what the checker flags (with the recorded answer, the length). Opens the thread, but does not read the sentences the checker passed. |
| **Secure** | Adds, unprompted, a line that asks for the message number after each fact. Uses the message numbers to read every sentence against its message, including those the checker passed, and finds the one that says the opposite of message 4. Keeps the customer's claims as hers ("Mira says"). Writes a corrected summary inside the length that still holds what the manager needs. |
| **Strong** | Explains why the checker cannot catch that sentence; notices a citation that passes only by coincidence; recomputes any figure that was worked out; explains why "in the material" is not the same as "right". |

## O3. Name what is wrong in an answer that looks right (exercise 3)

| Level | What you can see |
|---|---|
| **Not yet** | Marks most sentences "Fine". Judges by tone and fluency. |
| **Beginning** | Finds the wrong figures, but misses the facts that are not in the material and the breaches of the policy. |
| **Secure** | Finds the errors in answers A and B, of all three kinds, and gives each a reason that points to a message or a rule of the policy. |
| **Strong** | Reads the assistant's own notes as well as the reply; explains why the automatic check misses some errors (a figure in the thread with another meaning; an invented fact with no figure); says which error would cost the company most, and proposes a change to the brief that would prevent it. |

## O4. Decide what must not be pasted, and prepare a table so that it can be (exercise 4)

| Level | What you can see |
|---|---|
| **Not yet** | Would paste the doctor's note or the contact list; or refuses everything, including the published price list. Keeps every column of the table. |
| **Beginning** | Sorts the obvious snippets correctly, but is unsure about the "remove details" pile (the complaint, the orders table, the rota). Uses stand-ins for every column instead of leaving any out. |
| **Secure** | Sorts all eight snippets with a reason for each; prepares an extract that passes the check, keeping only what the question needs; maps the answer back with the key that stayed on the computer. Says that the employer's policy comes first. |
| **Strong** | Notices the health note hidden in the rota; suggests asking in general words instead of pasting; can state a three-line rule for their own team. |

## O5. Turn a request that worked into a template (exercise 5)

Exercise 5 is homework in the 90-minute plan, and checks itself; you see this objective in the room only in the half-day variant.

| Level | What you can see |
|---|---|
| **Not yet** | Copies the request unchanged, or deletes parts without putting placeholders in their place. |
| **Beginning** | Adds placeholders, but leaves a value of the case in (for example a name or the order number), or uses blanks such as `___`. |
| **Secure** | Named placeholders for the material, the people and the identifiers; nothing of the case left; the form and the limits kept. The template passes the check. |
| **Strong** | Adds a line telling the colleague what to fill in and what to check afterwards; names a second task in their own work that the template would serve. |
