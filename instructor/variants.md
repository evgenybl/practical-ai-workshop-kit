# Variants: 60 minutes, half a day, online

The same material, the same case and the same workbook, in three other shapes. All timings are **planned**: this kit has not yet been taught to a class, in any of these shapes. In every variant, send the single-file workbook (`participant/workbook-single-file.html`) by default: it opens with a double click and needs no folder.

---

## 60 minutes

For a lunchtime session or a slot inside a longer meeting. Everyone uses the recorded answers, so nobody waits for an assistant. Exercise 5 stays homework, as in the 90-minute plan.

| Block | Planned minutes | What changes from the 90-minute plan |
|---|---|---|
| Welcome and the case | 5 | Introduce the case in four sentences; give two minutes to read the policy only, and say the thread stays open all session. |
| Exercise 1 | 12 | Step 1 as planned (4 minutes reading, 2 sharing). Step 2: 5 minutes. Skip step 3; show *Compare with one strong brief* instead. The same debrief question. |
| Exercise 2 | 15 | Add the line, then everyone uses the recorded answer. Check it, then read the bullet about the loading against message 4: it passes every check and says the opposite. Skip writing the corrected summary. |
| Exercise 3 | 10 | Mark answer A only (seven sentences). Run the automatic check on A, which passes it on every rule, then look at B together on the screen. B and C become homework. |
| Exercise 4 | 12 | Part 1 as planned. Part 2: you demonstrate it on the screen (choose columns, prepare, check, map back with the example answer) instead of everyone doing it. |
| The close | 6 | Three questions from the check, chosen by you (questions 4, 6 and 7 cover the three ideas most worth keeping); set the rest, and exercise 5, as homework. |
| **Total** | **60** | |

**What you lose:** practice at writing a full brief with feedback from a live assistant, and the hands-on map-back. Say so, and point people to exercise 5 and the remaining questions as homework.

## Half a day (3 hours 30 minutes, with a break)

For a team that wants to leave with something they will use. The extra time goes to the participants' own work: they bring one real, non-sensitive task that they do every week. **Exercise 5 runs in the session** here, with its debrief question from the guide.

**Before the session**, ask each participant to bring one recurring writing task they would like help with, and to make sure it involves no personal or confidential data, or that they can describe it in general words.

| Block | Planned minutes | What it adds |
|---|---|---|
| Welcome, the case, and what people want from the session | 15 | Each person says the task they brought, in one sentence. |
| Exercise 1, in full, with step 3 | 25 | Pairs compare the replies their briefs produced, and say which part of the brief made the difference. |
| Exercise 2, in full | 30 | A second round: each person rewrites the summary for a different reader (the customer instead of the manager) and checks it. |
| Break | 15 | |
| Exercise 3, with answer C | 20 | All three answers. Each pair then rewrites the strong brief so that each error in answers A and C becomes impossible, and tests it. |
| Exercise 4, in full | 30 | The room drafts a three-line rule for its own team: what may go in, what must be cleaned first, what never. Check it against the employer's policy. |
| Exercise 5, in the session | 20 | Write the template, check it, and debrief: "Who in your team would use this template, and what would they need to know that is not in it yet?" |
| Your own task | 35 | Each person writes a five-part brief for the task they brought, gets an answer, checks it with the second card of the handout, and turns it into a template. Pairs then review each other's work against the rubric. |
| The ten-question check, and the close | 20 | Each person says the one template they will use this week. |
| **Total** | **210** | |

**Watch for:** the "own task" block drifting into real data. Remind the room of the third card before it starts.

## Online (90 minutes, on a video call)

The same plan, with three changes: the workbook goes out before the call, answers are collected in the chat, and the discussion exercises run in small breakout rooms.

**Before the session**

- Send `workbook-single-file.html` the day before, with one instruction: double-click it before the call. Send the printable case (`participant/case/customer-thread.txt` and `returns-policy.txt`) with it, so people can keep the case in view beside the call.
- Ask everyone to check which assistant, if any, their employer allows.
- Set up breakout rooms of two or three people.

**Changes, exercise by exercise**

| Block | Planned minutes | Online |
|---|---|---|
| Welcome and the case | 10 | Two extra minutes: check that everyone has the workbook open. Ask for a thumbs-up in the chat. |
| Exercise 1 | 17 | Collect the step 1 lists in the chat: everyone types at once, which is faster than taking turns to speak. |
| Exercise 2 | 19 | As in the room. For the debrief, ask each person to type one thing they changed. |
| Exercise 3 | 15 | Breakout rooms of two or three to mark answers A and B (8 minutes), then back to the main room for the debrief. Answer C is homework. |
| Exercise 4 | 20 | Part 1: ask for each snippet's pile in the chat, as "snippet number, pile". Part 2: individually. |
| The ten-question check, and the close | 9 | As in the room. Set exercise 5 as homework. |
| **Total** | **90** | |

**Watch for**

- **Cameras off.** Ask for answers in the chat by name: "Everyone, type the part of the brief you found hardest."
- **Real data in the shared chat.** Remind the room that the chat is not a safe place for anything they would not paste into an assistant.
- **Screen sharing.** Never ask a participant to share a screen with their answers on it unless they offer.
- **Silence after a question.** Online, the wait feels longer. Count to ten, not five, before you speak again.
